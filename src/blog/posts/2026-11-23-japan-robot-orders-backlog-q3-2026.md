**HUMI Japan Robotics Tracker · Q3 2026 · Data: Japan Robot Association (JARA), China customs**

## Key findings

- **Record backlog.** In July–September 2026, Japanese robot makers booked 67,003 robots in orders but produced only 51,414. That is **1.30 orders per robot built**, the highest quarter since at least 2017.
- **Ten quarters in a row.** Measured in yen, orders have exceeded production every quarter since Q2 2024. Over the last four quarters, orders ran about **¥218 billion** ahead of production, against about ¥137 billion at the 2021 peak.
- **The surge is broadening.** Chip mounters (machines that place components on circuit boards) drove about 59% of order growth over the past year. In Q3 2026, the lead passed to **robot arms**, where orders reached 1.34× production, the highest since the split begins in 2021.
- **China confirms it.** China's own customs data shows imports of industrial robots from Japan up **24.8%** in January–August 2026, in dollars.
- **The risk.** The last time this ratio was this high, in 2021, orders turned down within about a year and the industry went through a deep adjustment in 2023.

![Japan industrial robots: orders divided by production, 2017 to Q3 2026](/img/reports/jara-q3-2026-orders-vs-production.png)

## 1. The biggest gap between orders and production in ten years

JARA publishes quarterly orders and production for its member companies, which include Fanuc, Yaskawa, Kawasaki, Nachi, Denso Wave and the major chip-mounter makers. Dividing orders by production gives a simple measure of whether demand is running ahead of what factories can deliver: above 1.0, a backlog is building; below 1.0, it is being worked down.

We rebuilt the series from JARA's public quarterly releases back to 2017 (39 quarters) and checked every quarter against JARA's published annual totals.

Three things stand out:

- **Units:** Q3 2026's 1.30 is the highest single quarter in the series. The previous high was 1.23 in Q4 2020.
- **Value, 4-quarter rolling:** 1.22, also a series high. The 2021 boom peaked at 1.19.
- **Duration:** orders have exceeded production by value for ten consecutive quarters, the longest run in the data.

Production is rising (Q3 production value was up 26% year on year), but orders are rising faster (+47%).

## 2. What is driving it: chip mounters first, robot arms now

JARA's figures include two very different kinds of machine. **Robot arms** (JARA's "manipulating robots") are what most people picture: welding, handling and assembly robots. **Chip mounters** place electronic components on circuit boards, and their demand follows electronics and AI hardware spending.

![Orders by machine type and orders divided by production, 2021 to Q3 2026](/img/reports/jara-q3-2026-by-type.png)

Over the last four quarters:

| | Order growth (YoY) | Orders ÷ production |
|---|---|---|
| Chip mounters | +58% | 1.29 |
| Robot arms | +29% | 1.18 |
| **Total** | **+39%** | **1.22** |

Chip mounters accounted for about 59% of all order growth. In Q2 2026, chip-mounter orders (¥155.1 billion) were larger than robot-arm orders (¥146.8 billion) for the first time in this data.

**Q3 2026 changed the picture.** Robot-arm orders jumped to ¥177.9 billion (+41.5% year on year) against production of ¥132.7 billion, a ratio of 1.34. Meanwhile, the chip-mounter ratio eased to 1.15. The boom that started in electronics is now reaching general factory automation.

Shipment data points the same way. Domestic shipments to electronic-component makers rose 43% in Q3, while shipments to auto-parts makers rose 9%. By application, material handling (+29%) and semiconductor handling (+21%) grew fastest, while spot welding (−7%) and machine loading (−12%), typical car-plant jobs, declined.

## 3. Who is buying: Asia outside China is growing fastest

JARA's export values for January–September 2026, compared with the same period of 2025:

- **China:** ¥253.7 billion, +28%
- **Asia excluding China:** ¥197.4 billion, +38% (Q3 alone: +41%)
- **North America:** +5.5% in Q3 (+21% year to date)
- **Europe:** −1.6% in Q3 (+26% year to date)

The growth outside China (Taiwan, Korea, Southeast Asia, India) is consistent with electronics and semiconductor capacity being built across the region.

**Cross-check with China's customs data.** JARA's numbers are reported by the makers themselves, in yen. China's customs data, compiled monthly by the University of International Business and Economics, is independent and in dollars. It shows China imported **$775.8 million** of industrial robots from Japan in January–August 2026, **up 24.8%**, with Japan supplying 73% of China's robot imports. August alone was up 34%. Within that, robots built specifically for chip-fab handling were up 65% in August. Both sources tell the same story.

## 4. The 2021 lesson

The ratio has been here before. After COVID, orders surged in late 2020 and 2021, led by chip mounters (their ratio reached 1.43 in Q1 2021). Then:

- Chip-mounter orders peaked in early 2021 and were down 46% year on year by Q1 2023.
- Robot-arm orders peaked about 18 months later and fell 39% year on year by late 2023.
- The total ratio (in units) fell to 0.84 in Q4 2023, as makers worked through their backlogs with few new orders.

The sequence this time looks similar so far: chip mounters first, robot arms following. The difference is the driver. 2021 was a post-COVID restocking cycle; 2026 is tied to AI data-centre and semiconductor investment, plus labour shortages, which may be longer-lasting. The data cannot tell us which way it goes, but it gives two clear things to watch.

## What to watch in the Q4 2026 release (late January 2027)

1. **Robot-arm orders ÷ production.** If it stays above about 1.2, the broadening is real. A quick fall back toward 1.0 would look like 2022.
2. **Chip-mounter orders.** A second quarter of slowing would suggest the electronics cycle is peaking.
3. **Asia outside China.** Whether it keeps outgrowing China.

## Method and caveats

- Source: JARA quarterly statistics (member basis), 2017–Q3 2026. Q4 2017 and Q4 2018 are derived from annual totals minus the first three quarters. The type split (robot arms vs chip mounters) is available from 2021.
- Orders minus production is a proxy for backlog. It ignores cancellations, inventory and shipments from stock.
- JARA counts only member companies, and its "robot" totals include chip mounters.
- China customs figures are from the UIBE Digital Economy Lab's monthly China Industrial Robot Trade report (January–August 2026).
- All ratios and growth rates are HUMI calculations.
